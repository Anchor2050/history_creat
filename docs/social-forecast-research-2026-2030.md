# Social forecast research 2026–2030 — world-generation brief

Purpose: make research determine the world, let the world grow through institutions/material constraints, then let people live inside it. Technology is only one domain.

## High-confidence anchors visible in 2026

### 1. Household economy and the end of easy property assumptions
China enters the 15th Five-Year period with resilient output but weak domestic demand, continuing property adjustment and cautious households. Policy and outside forecasts both point toward consumption rebalancing and stronger safety nets. For fiction/simulation this means housing cannot remain a passive backdrop: rent, buying, parents' property, precautionary saving and willingness to change jobs all interact.

### 2. Aging, low fertility and care become ordinary economic infrastructure
The 15th Five-Year Plan explicitly treats aging and low fertility as central, expands childcare support, eldercare, long-term care and family-oriented policy. Research suggests low fertility is now sustained by preferences, costs, gender norms and family structure, so a subsidy must not mechanically create a baby. Parent care, fertility decisions and sibling/only-child burdens should consume real hours and money.

### 3. Housing shifts from expansion story to stock-city story
Urban policy emphasizes renewal of existing neighborhoods, rental markets, youth/new-citizen housing, accessibility and complete communities. A character's city life should therefore include old-building renovation, commute, community care, renting versus buying and uneven neighborhood public services—not only house prices.

### 4. Education/status hierarchy is being repriced
Education planning links program capacity to employment, expands professional degrees/strategic fields, adjusts disciplines faster and reforms evaluation toward contribution, integrity and practical outcomes. This can change the social meaning of a doctorate without making degrees worthless. Academic prestige, professional degrees, research autonomy and employer demand can diverge.

### 5. Longer working lives + flexible work alter the life course
Gradual retirement-age reform is already in force; from 2030 minimum pension contribution years begin rising. Policy is also extending protection for platform/new-form workers. This changes vacancy turnover, intergenerational job queues, parents' retirement timing, care availability and the boundary between a 'job' and a portfolio of work.

### 6. Intimacy and family formation become more optional and more difficult
Recent demographic research describes very low fertility intention, digital/offline hybrid sociality, gender differences and financial/care constraints. The model should not schedule marriage as an age milestone. Relationships can be delayed, non-marital, ended, repaired or deprioritized; children require a negotiated household capacity path.

### 7. Urbanization continues but geography differentiates
Urbanization reached 67.89% in 2025. Policy analysis expects more city-to-city and near-region movement, continued young-person concentration in major/provincial cities, stronger metropolitan areas, and population pressure in some smaller cities. 'Stay in Beijing / return home' should not be a binary morality play: provincial capitals, metro fringes, remote work, family proximity and public-service access create intermediate lives.

### 8. Climate/energy transition enters ordinary schedules
The 15th Five-Year Plan combines energy transition with adaptation to extreme weather. The world should express this as heat days, flooding, commute disruption, power/energy constraints, green jobs, building retrofit and insurance/municipal spending—not climate exposition.

## Scenario construction for 2027–2030

These are model scenarios, not factual predictions.

**Baseline rebalancing (45% model prior):** property adjustment continues but social protection, urban renewal and consumption support gradually improve confidence. Care services expand unevenly. Labor market remains competitive. Green and advanced-industry jobs grow.

**Weak-demand long adjustment (25%):** property/household confidence remains weak; employers guard headcount; young adults delay household formation; family transfers and stable public-sector-like positions gain subjective value; geographic mobility becomes more selective.

**Productivity + social dividend (15%):** productivity gains are accompanied by stronger social protection/training and cheaper/better public services. People gain genuine leisure/choice rather than only higher output. This is the optimistic branch and must remain possible.

**Fragmented transition (15%):** new industries grow but gains concentrate; care, housing, regional and employment inequalities remain; some institutions modernize while others preserve old evaluation systems. Social resentment/status mismatch become important without requiring catastrophe.

Weights are world-generation priors chosen for exploration. They are not source-estimated probabilities.

## How research is allowed to enter history

Research never directly writes plot. It changes state variables and institutions. Example chain:

aging + delayed retirement → parent retires later → less free grandparent care → Chen's family-care hours rise → she refuses overtime → promotion probability changes → team redistributes work → another person's opportunity changes.

Or:

urban renewal + weak property expectations → Lin keeps renting near transit → preserves geographic mobility → can accept lower-paid autonomous research role → friendship/status comparison with Chen changes.

Or:

education evaluation reform + tighter academic labor market → university changes hiring rubric → Xu's reproducibility portfolio becomes legible → still competes with candidates carrying stronger conventional publications → no guaranteed reward.

## Convergence rule

Iteration continues until additional research or replay mostly changes local texture rather than the causal trunk. A history is considered structurally converged only when:

1. major outcomes survive at least three plausible social scenarios;
2. scenario-sensitive outcomes are explicitly marked contingent;
3. no major life event exists only because the author wanted a theme;
4. family, housing, work, institution, city and relationship histories all leave persistent state;
5. later choices reinterpret earlier choices without retroactively changing what actors knew.
