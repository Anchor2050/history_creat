# Research 2035 anchor pass 02 — care, gender/family, city divergence

Status: research-to-mechanism pass. This document continues pass 01 and attacks the three unresolved trunk-changing uncertainties. It does not declare forecasts to be facts.

## 1. Observed baseline versus project assumptions

### Observed baseline (2025 official statistics)
China ended 2025 with 1.40489 billion people, 7.92 million births and 11.31 million deaths. Population aged 60+ reached 323.38 million (23.0%); 65+ reached 223.65 million (15.9%). Urbanization reached 67.89%. Employment was 725.04 million. Basic pension participation was 1.07599 billion and basic medical insurance participation 1.33068 billion. There were 39,000 elderly-care institutions and 7.68 million elderly-care beds. December 2025 second-hand housing prices fell year-on-year in all 70 large and medium cities in the NBS sample.

Source: National Bureau of Statistics, Statistical Communiqué of the PRC on 2025 National Economic and Social Development, 2026-02-28; NBS 2025 population release, 2026-01-19.

These are observed anchors. They do not tell us how much family care time is displaced by formal services, which cities win the next decade, or how unpaid care is divided inside couples.

### Project assumptions to stress, not facts
- Formal care expands unevenly and rarely converts one-for-one into family free time.
- Major cities retain high-value opportunity density while some secondary cities offer cheaper housing and easier family proximity.
- Marriage/childbearing choices interact with employment continuity, housing, grandparents, migration and gendered unpaid labor.
- The relevant historical variable is not simply whether a service exists, but whether a household can convert it into reliable hours, mobility and bargaining power.

## 2. Care attack: beds are not hours

The first pass risked treating care expansion as a scalar relief parameter. Replace that with a conversion chain:

formal capacity -> eligibility/access -> affordability -> geographic proximity -> scheduling reliability -> quality/trust -> family hours actually released -> whether those hours can be converted into work/migration/rest.

This makes two superficially contradictory futures compatible. Elder-care supply can expand strongly while middle-aged children still lose promotion and migration options. Institutional supply may handle bathing, meals or daytime supervision while appointments, emergencies, paperwork, emotional labor and coordination remain with family. A bed count therefore cannot directly subtract care hours.

For Chen, the strongest counterfactual is no longer “long-term care solves the problem.” It is: a reliable local service removes enough routine hours to keep her employed, but not enough contingency responsibility to make a cross-city promotion safe. Her 2034 refusal can survive substantial care-policy improvement while becoming less inevitable.

A stronger-care branch should exist: if coverage, trusted home/community services and medical-care coordination improve together, Chen can accept the promotion. The historical consequence then moves downstream: she buys mobility with money and institutional support, but someone still performs coordination; the question becomes whether that labor is fairly paid or invisibly transferred to a spouse, sibling, low-paid worker or elderly parent.

## 3. Gender/family attack: household bargains must enter the causal graph

Family formation cannot be represented as a romance flag. Add household bargaining state:
- each partner's income and career continuity;
- housing ownership/lease security and commute geography;
- proximity and health of both sets of parents;
- childcare/care service availability and reliability;
- pregnancy/childbearing career interruption where relevant;
- default assignment of night care, appointment coordination and school logistics;
- whose job is treated as movable when two careers conflict;
- exit options: savings, welfare, housing rights, employability and social support.

The key mechanism is **asymmetric reversibility**. Two partners may agree to a temporary specialization (“you step back for two years”) while the labor market, professional networks and pension accumulation make the concession hard to reverse. A household can rationally choose an arrangement that later becomes a power imbalance without either person intending harm.

This attacks the attractive autonomy story directly. Lin's mobility is only an asset while no relationship or care arrangement makes moving costly. If she enters a serious partnership, “I can move” becomes a joint bargaining problem. Her 2032 missed opportunity should therefore have at least three plausible causes across branches: fear/status attachment; partner-location constraint; family-care proximity. The trunk should retain the more general result: **options decay when institutions or relationships make their exercise progressively costly**.

Goodness test: do not reward the character who refuses all dependency. Mutual dependence can be good. The moral failure to track is concealed cost transfer — one person's clean career line purchased with another person's interrupted career, unpaid coordination or foregone mobility.

## 4. City-divergence attack: migration is a bundle exchange

Do not model Beijing versus destination city as salary alone. A move exchanges a bundle:

salary + opportunity density + professional network + institutional prestige + spouse opportunities + housing cost/security + commute time + school/childcare access + elder-care distance + medical access + welfare portability + future option value.

Therefore cheaper housing can fail to create autonomy if it destroys professional outside options; a lower nominal salary can increase autonomy if it sharply lowers rent/commute and restores family support; Beijing can remain attractive even with weak housing returns because dense professional networks preserve future conversion opportunities.

The trunk claim should be city-agnostic: **migration decisions increasingly price access to several institutions at once, and the winning city differs by life stage.** Early career may favor opportunity density; childrearing or elder-care years may favor proximity, housing and time; later career may again favor network density if accumulated expertise is portable.

This also prevents the project from declaring a generic “young people leave megacities” future. The 2025 baseline still shows continuing national urbanization, while housing adjustment is broad. City sorting is a conditional branch, not an observed national exodus.

## 5. Combined stress cases for 2030–2035

### C1 — strong care institutions, weak labor market
Chen gains reliable community/home care. Her weekly care load falls enough to protect employment, yet the cross-city promotion remains unattractive because the receiving office offers fragile tenure and poor spouse/family coordination. Result: care relief changes texture and probability, not the institutional-asynchrony trunk.

### C2 — weak care institutions, strong regional opportunity
A secondary city develops a strong professional cluster and cheaper housing. Lin moves; Chen cannot because parental care is location-bound. Earlier status rankings reverse again: the person with lower prior income obtains more geographic option value. Trunk survives.

### C3 — partnership and child branch
Lin forms a partnership in which both careers initially look symmetric. Childcare shock plus different employer flexibility makes her career the “temporarily movable” one. The temporary concession persists. Her lost option is now produced by household bargaining plus organizational rules rather than fear alone. This is a stronger and less flattering causal structure than pass 01.

### C4 — care boom with hidden labor transfer
Publicly subsidized care expands, but low-paid care workers absorb irregular schedules and emotional load. Chen's mobility improves. Goodness filter fails any narrative that calls this an uncomplicated social victory. The cost has moved down the labor hierarchy.

### C5 — generous family policy with organizational penalty
Childcare support improves while promotion systems still price uninterrupted availability. Family policy reduces cash/time cost without eliminating career scarring. This is institutional asynchrony in a new domain.

### C6 — no dramatic shock
Housing stays softer, care supply improves gradually, careers remain competitive, no singular AI discontinuity occurs. The same characters still face slow repricing of credentials, mobility, care and reliability. This null-shock case is important: the trunk does not require spectacle.

## 6. What this pass changes

The care mechanism is retained but rewritten from “care consumes time” to **care coordination creates a contingent-time liability**. Formal services can reduce its mean while leaving a high-variance tail that still constrains migration and promotion.

The autonomy mechanism is retained but rewritten as **reversible-option capacity**. Cash, housing, health, welfare, professional networks and reciprocal relationships jointly determine whether an option can actually be exercised.

A new trunk candidate enters: **private bargains can become institutionalized by unequal reversibility**. A temporary family compromise can harden into career, pension, skill and bargaining-power divergence.

City divergence is demoted from a possible national direction to a sorting mechanism. Different cities price different bundles; life stage changes the weights.

## 7. Truth / goodness / beauty

Truth: stronger because care, migration and family choices now pass through access, reliability, geography, bargaining and option-value channels rather than trend slogans.

Goodness: stronger because public improvement can still externalize costs onto spouses, siblings or care workers. Mutual dependence is allowed; concealed transfer is the target.

Beauty: a promising long echo now appears. A 2027 argument about “who is ahead” can be reinterpreted in 2034–2035 when income, mobility and care obligations reverse rank. The object that persists is not a winner but the changing exchange rate among life resources.

## 8. Convergence implication

These three attacks no longer overturn the main mechanism. They change who moves, who bears care, and which opportunity is lost. Institutional asynchrony, conversion lag, contingent care liability and reversible-option capacity survive all six combined cases.

One major convergence test remains before declaration: replay the core characters under these combined cases and explicitly delete any event that only works because the author knows the future. If the long causal skeleton survives that local-information replay and a no-dramatic-shock branch, the 2035 trunk is close to convergence.
