# 2035 forecast research — pass 01

Status: research-grounded world input, not canonical character history.

## Epistemic layers

### Observed / adopted policy anchors (2026)
- The 15th Five-Year Plan treats low fertility and aging as structural population issues and calls for lower childrearing costs, expanded eldercare, care for disabled/demented older adults, and long-term-care insurance.
- Employment policy emphasizes structural mismatch, lifelong skills, flexible/new-form work protection, and monitoring the employment effects of external change and new technology.
- Social protection policy points toward broader unemployment/work-injury coverage, portability, and higher participation among flexible and new-form workers.
- Urban renewal policy explicitly shifts development toward stock renewal, rental and保障 housing, youth/new-resident housing difficulties, public services and resilient cities; it gives a 2035 vision rather than a return to pure land/property expansion.

### Institutional outlook anchors
- IMF 2026: medium-term growth decelerates as labor force declines, investment returns weaken and productivity growth slows; property contraction and trade tension are important downside risks.
- World Bank 2026: domestic demand and property adjustment remain weak; stronger social protection could reduce precautionary saving.
- These are outlooks, not facts about 2035.

## World-model consequences to 2035

The model should not extrapolate a single index. It should propagate linked mechanisms.

1. **Care squeeze**: parents age while protagonists are in their 30s/40s. Care hours, proximity, sibling bargaining and long-term-care coverage can dominate career choice even when income rises.
2. **Safety-net option value**: unemployment, health and care protection can increase a person's ability to reject a bad job. Welfare therefore changes bargaining power, not only consumption.
3. **Housing becomes heterogeneous infrastructure**: tenure is no longer a universal success scalar. Location, commute, care proximity, rental security, inherited housing and liquidity can point in opposite directions.
4. **Long working lives + changing skills**: delayed retirement and lifelong training can simultaneously reduce near-term vacancy turnover and increase later-career reinvention.
5. **City divergence**: metropolitan job density can coexist with high commute/care costs; provincial-city opportunity can coexist with weaker specialist networks. Migration is an option with switching costs.
6. **Credential unbundling**: degrees remain signals but occupational value increasingly depends on problem ownership, regulated responsibility, verification, client/institution trust and continuous learning.
7. **Household caution loop**: weak property expectations + care uncertainty + insecure work can raise precautionary saving, reducing consumption and feeding weak demand. Stronger safety nets can partly break the loop.
8. **External-fragmentation transmission**: trade/geopolitical shocks should reach characters through employer orders, budgets, city sectors and hiring—not exposition.
9. **Climate as time shock**: heat/flood events mainly matter through commute failure, elder vulnerability, health costs, insurance/public services and organizational disruption.
10. **AI as one multiplier**: automation changes task prices and training ladders, but its life impact is mediated by all mechanisms above.

## 2031–2035 scenario envelope

Do not attach claimed probabilities. Use a correlated ensemble:

- `managed_rebalancing`: stronger safety net, gradual consumption shift, property no longer primary household wealth engine, moderate productivity diffusion.
- `long_adjustment`: weak demand/property drag persists, budgets tight, hiring selective, households defensive.
- `productive_inclusive`: productivity gains combine with broader welfare/training and stronger worker mobility.
- `productive_unequal`: high productivity but gains concentrate; verification/care/training needs rise without matching bargaining power.
- `fragmented_external`: trade and supply-chain shocks create sector/city divergence and volatile employer budgets.
- `care_heavy_transition`: aging/illness shocks outrun service expansion; family time becomes the scarce resource.

## What can enter the historical trunk now

Robust mechanism candidates:
- material success does not imply time autonomy;
- socially valuable work need not receive a market price;
- welfare and housing conditions alter the feasible set of moral choices;
- care can reverse migration and career decisions years after the original choice;
- organizational authority does not guarantee budget authority;
- the old bundle of degree → stable job → home → family → secure retirement becomes less reliable as a single life script.

Still conditional:
- verification necessarily becomes a high-paid profession;
- AI produces a particular unemployment rate;
- any protagonist definitely buys a home, marries, has a child, leaves Beijing, or becomes management;
- Chen can permanently institutionalize paid training;
- Lin's autonomy strategy necessarily outperforms Chen's material strategy.
