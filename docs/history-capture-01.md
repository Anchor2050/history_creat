# History capture 01 — from replay nodes to lived history

## Why this pass exists

The previous pass had a strong 18-node causal spine, but a list of important decisions is still not history. History needs intervals: ordinary weeks, material drift, institutions, relationships, external waves, inherited memory, and later reinterpretation.

This pass therefore stops asking only “what is the next dramatic choice?” and captures five continuous periods from March 2026 to January 2030.

## Captured history

### I. 2026 spring — 毕业以前，世界还很小

The world is still measured in small things: a submission window, advisor attention, a family transfer, printing, reimbursement, a shared dorm dinner. Chen's legitimate need for an administrative exception removes an option from Lin. Lin then makes her own bad choice by confusing autonomy with isolation and wastes a month.

The historical seed is already present: **one person's reasonable survival choice changes another person's feasible set**. Nobody experiences this as “history.”

### II. 2026 summer–2027 spring — 第一次上升

Chen graduates and leaves the university. Her material life improves first: more income, clearer work boundaries, access to a company. Lin remains on the slower research path.

The difference enters friendship through rent, consumption and time. Chen's mistake is mundane: during a housing conversation she uses her new income as an implicit standard for Lin's life. The later apology does not erase the wound.

This is the point an outsider would later call Chen's victory. The captured history refuses to freeze that judgment.

### III. 2027–2028 — 第二次毕业开始了，却没人给它发证书

Agentic work makes routine implementation cheaper. The important historical change appears as a pricing mismatch: organizations can count output immediately, while verification, scope and responsibility are slower to value.

Chen spends billable time learning those slower skills and misses a promotion. Lin stops trying to beat machines at technical virtuosity. Gu gives Xu failure-audit work that makes his publication record look worse.

At the time, none of them knows whether these are wise sacrifices. Calling them “visionary” would falsify the history.

### IV. 2028–2029 — 答案便宜以后

An AI-assisted client failure makes verification commercially visible. Chen becomes useful because she can own a boundary and a failure. Lin rejects a prestigious role whose real job is invisible verification labor. Xu's failed-reproduction archive becomes evidence that he can distinguish a plausible answer from a trustworthy one.

The same social need produces unequal outcomes: verification can become a moat for one worker and unpaid/invisible labor for another. The technology wave therefore creates opportunity and exploitation at once.

### V. 2029–2030 — 两种自由

Xu delays Lin's project by identifying a provenance gap. Chen now has hiring authority and refuses unpaid trial work, costing her team a project. The moral return of the early years is institutional: people who once depended on exceptions now decide whether transition costs are pushed onto younger people.

At an old-friends gathering the question “who is doing best?” returns. Lin and Chen decline the premise. Chen has more purchasing and organizational power; Lin has more power to refuse claims and work she does not believe in. Neither life proves the other wrong.

## The history we have actually caught

The strongest historical object is no longer “AI changes three careers.” It is:

> **A generation enters adulthood while the price system around knowledge work changes. Execution gets cheaper before institutions know how to price judgment, verification, responsibility, training and autonomy. The transition becomes personal because somebody must pay its costs: students, junior workers, clients, firms, friends or families. Characters reveal themselves when they later acquire enough power to decide who pays next.**

This gives the project four interacting historical lines:

- **material:** precarity → income → repricing → authority;
- **epistemic:** execution → verification → responsibility;
- **relational:** shared scarcity → comparison wound → repair → incomparable freedoms;
- **institutional:** receiving exceptions → allocating opportunities → deciding whether costs flow downward.

## Reality anchor at the start of capture

The 2026 environment is no longer speculative in one important respect: policy and industry are explicitly moving toward large-scale AI/agent deployment, new forms of human-machine work, industrial upgrading, and employment adaptation. The future consequences remain uncertain. Deep replay should therefore treat the direction of pressure as observed by late 2026 while keeping 2027–2030 outcomes scenario-dependent.

## Next depth target

The next pass should deepen **inside this captured history**, not branch back out. It should reconstruct the missing connective tissue between these five periods:

1. monthly material state for the three younger characters;
2. changing social-network edges and trust after each wound/help event;
3. concrete institutional decisions that translate era pressure into jobs, pay and training;
4. family/city/housing consequences, so work is not the whole life;
5. one or more decisions where the historically “right” adaptation later turns out partly wrong.

The purpose is to make the history capable of surprising the replay engine without losing causal continuity.
