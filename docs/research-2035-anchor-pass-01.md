# 2035 research anchor pass 01

Status: research-grounded world input, not a forecast claim.
Date of research pass: 2026-10-11.

## Epistemic layers

### Observed anchors
- 2025 population: 1.40489b; births 7.92m; deaths 11.31m; age 60+ 323.38m; age 65+ 223.65m; urbanization 67.89%.
- 2025 per-capita disposable income: RMB 43,377; median RMB 36,231. Wage income remained the largest component; net property income grew much more slowly than wages/transfers.
- Statutory retirement ages began gradual increases in 2025. From 2030, minimum contribution years for monthly basic pension begin rising from 15 toward 20 years, by six months per year.

### Policy commitments / targets (not guaranteed outcomes)
- 2026-2030 employment strategy explicitly treats structural job mismatch as a central problem; it links education supply to labor demand, calls for lifelong skills training, employment impact assessment, service-sector expansion (including eldercare/childcare), and AI-human collaborative work.
- Urban-renewal plan: by 2035, target is a more mature renewal system and substantial progress in city structure, livability, resilience, green transition and governance.
- National health plan targets life expectancy 80 by 2030 and frames 2030 as groundwork for Healthy China 2035.
- Carbon/energy plans target 2030 carbon-intensity reduction and non-fossil energy expansion, laying groundwork for 2035 climate commitments.

### Institution forecasts / risks
- World Bank July 2026: 4.4% growth in 2026 and 4.3% in 2027; gradual consumption rebalancing; property adjustment and cautious consumers remain important risks; stronger social protection could reduce precautionary saving. Low-carbon transition creates new jobs but skill gaps can limit inclusive gains.
- IMF Feb 2026: medium-term growth expected to decelerate with declining labor force, lower investment returns and slower productivity; property contraction and trade escalation are downside risks; stronger social protection and consumption-led rebalancing are recommended.

### Project scenarios, not sourced probabilities
The project must not extrapolate a single 2035 path. Use correlated regimes:
1. managed_rebalancing: social protection and services improve gradually; property ceases to be the default ladder; slower but more consumption-oriented growth.
2. long_adjustment: property/weak-demand drag persists; organizations ration training and junior entry; family buffers matter more.
3. inclusive_productivity: productivity gains coexist with stronger portable welfare, training and service provision; conversion from skill to autonomy becomes easier for more people.
4. unequal_productivity: output rises but bargaining power/training/welfare lag; high-skill insiders gain while junior ladders and invisible labor worsen.
5. fragmented_external: trade/geopolitical shocks periodically hit export/service budgets and migration opportunities; domestic regional divergence increases.
6. care_squeeze: aging/illness outruns service expansion locally; household care time becomes a binding constraint even when cash improves.

These regimes can combine; care_squeeze and external fragmentation are overlays rather than mutually exclusive macro worlds.

## Mechanisms that survive the research pass

### 1. Demography acts through time, not only population totals
Aging should enter character lives as parent-care hours, hospital coordination, distance-to-family, sibling bargaining, paid-care affordability and retirement timing. More eldercare supply can reduce but not erase household care. Longer healthy life can also increase parents' independence and work, so aging is not coded as automatic catastrophe.

### 2. Welfare is an option-producing asset
Portable health/pension/unemployment protection changes whether a character can refuse a bad job, move city, retrain, or absorb a gap in earnings. Treat welfare as part of the feasible set, not background scenery.

### 3. Housing changes from universal success token to heterogeneous option bundle
Track housing as: shelter cost, commute, liquidity, family transfer, migration lock-in, school/care access and exposure to neighborhood renewal. Ownership can raise security while lowering mobility; renting can preserve option value but increase tenure risk. No universal sign.

### 4. Education becomes a recurrent conversion problem
Degree -> first job is insufficient. Track degree/skill -> job access -> bargaining power -> retraining capacity. Policy explicitly links programs and enrollment to labor outcomes; this can improve matching while also making fields and credentials more exposed to administrative repricing.

### 5. Longer working lives alter queues and family calendars
Delayed retirement should not be modeled simply as fewer youth jobs. It changes senior turnover, mentoring capacity, pension timing, grandparent care availability and the duration of multi-generation work/care overlap.

### 6. Green transition is labor and place transition
Energy/climate policy can create engineering/service jobs and skill premiums while also producing regional and sectoral displacement. Extreme heat/flooding changes commute, health, care and city infrastructure reliability. Keep job creation and displacement together.

### 7. Macro weakness is transmitted through organizational budgets
Property/consumption/trade shocks should reach characters via hiring freezes, training budgets, client demand, grant/contract timing, family transfers and confidence—not speeches about GDP.

## New core historical hypothesis to test, not canonize yet

The 2026-2035 cohort does not merely face a breakup of the old 'success bundle'. It increasingly faces **asynchronous institutions**: education, employers, housing, welfare, care systems and families reprice adulthood at different speeds. A person can adapt correctly to one system and become more exposed to another.

Examples:
- Chen converts skill into income, then income makes family care expectations and organizational responsibility heavier.
- Lin preserves mobility/autonomy through lower fixed commitments, but weak welfare or unstable housing can make that autonomy fragile.
- Xu converts verification skill into institutional trust, but if job classifications/pay lag the need for verification, social value does not convert into market value.

The history engine should therefore track both conversion and **conversion lag**: the years between social usefulness and recognized price/status/rights.
