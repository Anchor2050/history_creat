# Social-world Monte Carlo 01 — 20,000 stress worlds

This is an engineering stress test of the converged social thesis. Coefficients are transparent assumptions, not estimated parameters; the percentages below must never be presented as real-world probabilities.

Seed: `20261011`. Draws: `20,000`.

## Overall stress outcomes

- Chen achieves modeled material income exit: **47.1%**
- Chen also retains modeled time sovereignty: **15.4%**
- Chen has budget room to preserve humane paid training: **16.1%**
- Lin retains high modeled option value: **26.0%**
- Xu receives clear market reward for verification: **21.5%**
- Chen gets material exit while lacking time sovereignty: **37.8%**
- Chen gets material exit while lacking enough organizational budget for humane training: **33.3%**

The low absolute rates are not forecasts. Their use is comparative: the model makes it much easier for income to improve than for money, time, care, organizational budget and bargaining power to improve together.

## What this falsifies

A clean 'Chen escapes precarity and becomes a humane manager' arc is too fragile. Even in this intentionally simple stress model, income improvement frequently coexists with low time sovereignty or insufficient organizational slack to institutionalize her ethics.

A clean 'Xu's verification skill is vindicated by the market' arc is also too fragile. Epistemic value and market reward must remain separate state variables.

## What survives

The simulation strengthens the decomposition thesis: success dimensions routinely fail to align. The historically interesting object is therefore the **conversion problem** between resources:

- money -> time;
- expertise -> bargaining power;
- housing -> mobility/security;
- public welfare -> practical autonomy;
- moral intention -> organizational capacity;
- degree -> labor-market position;
- family closeness -> care obligation;
- technical usefulness -> reward.

A person's life is shaped by which conversions work and which fail.

## Next historical extraction rule

Future replay should record every major conversion attempt as one of:

1. successful conversion;
2. blocked conversion;
3. conversion with hidden cost;
4. delayed conversion;
5. conversion that benefits one actor by transferring cost to another.

This gives the history miner a more durable object than promotions, crises or AI milestones.
